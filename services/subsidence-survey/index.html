Subsidence survey in Leicester

A subsidence survey tries to answer three questions: is the house moving, why, and what should be done about it.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

A survey is the first step when cracks look serious. It is carried out by a structural engineer or a surveyor with experience of ground movement. The aim is to find the cause, not just to describe the damage.

What is included

  • A visual inspection inside and out, recording the cracks, their width, direction and position
  • A check of the house's age, construction and foundations, with trial pits dug beside the building if needed
  • Assessment of nearby trees, drains, ground levels and past alterations
  • Soil sampling or testing where the cause is unclear
  • A plan for monitoring, if the movement needs measuring over time
  • A written report with findings and recommendations

Surveyor, engineer or insurer's adjuster?

If you are claiming on buildings insurance, your insurer will usually appoint a loss adjuster who arranges the investigation. If you are buying, or paying privately, you choose. A chartered structural engineer is the usual choice for a subsidence report. See Claiming on Buildings Insurance and Buying a House with Cracks.

What a good report covers

  1. Description of the property, the cracks and the evidence
  2. Diagnosis: subsidence, heave, settlement or something else, and the likely cause
  3. Recommendations: monitoring, tree work, drain repair, stabilisation or repair
  4. Limits: what the surveyor could not see or confirm

How long it takes

The visit often takes a couple of hours. The report may follow within days to a couple of weeks. If monitoring is recommended, the full answer can take a year because cracks need measuring through a full cycle of the seasons. See Crack Monitoring.

What it costs

Engineer's reports commonly cost somewhere in the region of £500 to £1,500 depending on the property and the scope, though prices vary. See typical costs.

Questions to ask before you instruct anyone

  • Are you a chartered engineer or surveyor, and do you carry professional indemnity insurance?
  • Have you dealt with subsidence on clay soils before?
  • What is included in the fee, and what would be extra?
  • Will the report be suitable for my insurer or my buyer?

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a survey if I'm claiming on insurance?

Your insurer will usually arrange one. Check with them before paying for your own, as commissioning a report first can complicate the claim.

How soon can a surveyor visit?

That depends on the firm and the time of year. Autumn after a dry summer is busy.

Will the report tell me what it will cost to fix?

It should recommend what work is needed. Detailed pricing usually comes from contractors afterwards.
