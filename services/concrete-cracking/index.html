Concrete cracking and sunken slabs

Cracked floors, sunken drives and tilting patios are often a symptom of what is happening in the ground underneath.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Concrete is strong in compression but cracks easily when the ground moves. It can also crack for ordinary reasons that have nothing to do with subsidence, so it is worth finding out which applies.

Common causes

  • Normal shrinkage as concrete cures, producing fine cracks or cracks along planned joints
  • Thermal movement from heat and cold
  • Poor preparation: thin slabs, weak sub-base or missing reinforcement
  • Ground movement: clay shrinking or swelling, tree roots and washed-out soil
  • Leaking drains or water pipes under the slab
  • Heavy loads that the slab was never designed for

When to worry

  • Cracks inside the house floor, particularly if walls are also cracking
  • Slabs that have dropped or tilted, creating steps or gaps against the building
  • Voids you can feel or hear beneath the slab
  • A patio or drive sinking next to the house where a drain runs

Repair options

OptionWhen it is used
Fill and sealFine, stable cracks that are not moving
Stitching or reinforcingCracks that need tying across
Void filling or slab liftingVoids beneath slabs, by grout or resin. See Resin Injection
Cut out and replaceBadly damaged sections or a poor sub-base
Foundation workWhen the building itself is moving

Insurance

Buildings insurance often excludes drives, paths and patios unless the main building is damaged too, so check the wording. See Extensions, Bays and Garden Walls for how it affects garden walls and outbuildings.

What to do next

Take photos and measurements. If the cracks are in the house floor or the slab is close to the building, treat it as a possible subsidence sign and read Reading Cracks in Your Walls.

Frequently asked questions

Is a cracked concrete floor always subsidence?

No. Shrinkage and poor construction are common causes. Cracks that grow, or occur with wall cracks, deserve investigation.

Can sunken concrete be lifted?

Sometimes. Resin or grout is injected beneath to fill voids and raise the slab. It only works if the cause has been dealt with.

Does insurance cover my drive?

Often not as standard. Check your policy.
