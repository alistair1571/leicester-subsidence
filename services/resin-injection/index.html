Resin injection for subsidence

A low-disruption way of strengthening ground under foundations, used where it suits the soil and the problem.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Resin injection uses a structural resin that is injected into the ground through small holes, and expands to fill voids, compact loose soil and lift or stabilise foundations. It can be quicker and cleaner than digging, but it is not right for every house.

How it works

  1. Small holes are drilled through the floor or around the outside of the building.
  2. Tubes are inserted to the target depth.
  3. Resin is injected and expands, filling voids and compacting weak soil. Monitoring equipment checks the effect.
  4. Holes are filled and the surface made good.

When it can suit

  • Loose or washed-out ground under shallow foundations, for example after a drain leak
  • Voids under floor slabs, paths and patios
  • Situations where digging is difficult or disruptive

When it might not

  • Where the cause is a tree still drawing water from clay, since the resin does not stop the soil shrinking
  • Where the ground needs a deeper, load-bearing solution
  • Where the soil is very soft and the resin may not behave predictably

It is normally one option among several. See Underpinning and Screw Piling for comparison.

Cost and time

Resin injection is often quoted in the region of £5,000 to £15,000 for a house, depending on the area treated. Work usually takes a few days. Prices vary, and ground investigation affects the scope.

Questions to ask

  • Why is resin suitable for my soil and my problem?
  • Will the effect be monitored and recorded?
  • What guarantee is offered, and who stands behind it?

Frequently asked questions

Is resin injection better than underpinning?

It is a different tool. It can be faster and less disruptive, but is only suitable for certain ground conditions and problems.

Is it disruptive?

Usually less so than digging. There is drilling and some mess, but most people can stay at home.

Will it fix a tree problem?

No. If clay is shrinking because of roots, the cause needs to be dealt with as well.
