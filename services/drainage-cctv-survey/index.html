Drainage CCTV survey

A camera on a cable goes along your drains and shows what is happening where you cannot see.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

If cracks appear in a house, one of the first questions is whether the drains are involved. A CCTV survey answers it. It also gives insurers evidence to include or rule out the drains as a cause.

How a survey works

  1. Access is gained through manholes, inspection chambers or gullies.
  2. A small camera on a flexible rod or crawler is pushed through the pipes, recording video as it goes.
  3. Defects are logged with their location: cracks, broken or displaced joints, roots, collapsed sections, sags and signs of leakage.
  4. A report and recording are produced, often with a plan of the drain run.

What it can find

  • Cracked or crushed pipes
  • Joints that have slipped, letting water out and roots in
  • Root intrusion
  • Pipes out of line or at the wrong fall
  • Unrecorded connections or old drains

What it cannot do

A survey shows the inside of the pipe. It does not directly show the ground beneath. If defects are found close to the foundations, the engineer will consider whether washout could have happened. See Leaking Drains and Washed-Out Ground.

Who is responsible for repairs

Drains within your boundary serving only your property are usually your responsibility. Shared drains and sewers beyond the boundary are normally the water company's, which for most of Leicestershire is Severn Trent Water. Check with them before paying for work on a shared pipe.

Cost

A domestic CCTV drain survey commonly costs about £250 to £600, depending on length and complexity. Your insurer may pay if the survey is part of a claim. See Subsidence Repair Costs.

Frequently asked questions

Will a drain survey show if the drain caused subsidence?

It shows defects in the pipe. An engineer then judges whether they could have caused soil loss near the foundations.

Do I need to be home?

Usually someone needs to give access to manholes and gullies. The survey itself often takes an hour or two.

Can I use the recording for my insurance claim?

Yes. Ask for the video and report, and send both to your insurer.
