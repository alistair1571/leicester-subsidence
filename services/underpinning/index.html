Underpinning in Leicester

Underpinning strengthens or deepens a building's foundations so they rest on firmer ground. It is a big job, so it should be the answer only when simpler fixes will not work.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Underpinning is often what people think of when they hear "subsidence", but many subsidence cases never need it. If the cause is a tree or a leaking drain, dealing with that may be enough. Underpinning is for when the ground itself cannot be relied on to hold the foundations.

When it is used

  • The foundations are too shallow for the ground, and movement continues after other causes are dealt with
  • Ground has been washed out, or is weak or made-up
  • The house has moved significantly and needs to be stabilised
  • Foundations need deepening for an extension or loft conversion

The main methods

MethodWhat happens
Traditional mass-concrete underpinningSections under the walls are dug out in a set sequence and filled with concrete to a deeper, firmer level
Mini-piled underpinningSmall-diameter piles are driven or bored to firm ground and connected by beams under the walls
Screw pilesSteel piles are screwed into the ground to carry the load. See Screw Piling
Resin injectionResin is injected to strengthen the ground without digging. See Resin Injection

What to expect

  • Disruption: noise, dust, access to the outside walls and some digging inside. Most families can stay in the house
  • Duration: often a few weeks, depending on the extent
  • Building control: usually needs building regulations approval, and sometimes party wall notices if you share a wall
  • Afterwards: cracks are repaired once the structure is stable, and decorating follows

Cost

Traditional underpinning is often quoted at roughly £1,500 to £2,600 per metre of foundation. A typical house job can fall in the range of £10,000 to £30,000, and complicated jobs can exceed £50,000. These are broad figures, and quotes vary a lot. If the work is part of an insurance claim, the insurer normally pays, less any excess. See Subsidence Repair Costs.

Questions to ask

  • Why is underpinning needed rather than fixing the cause?
  • How much of the house will be underpinned?
  • Is the design by a chartered structural engineer and signed off by building control?
  • What guarantee do I get, and will it transfer to a buyer?

Frequently asked questions

Does subsidence always need underpinning?

No. Many cases are resolved by removing the cause, such as repairing a drain or managing a tree. Underpinning is for when the ground cannot hold the foundations.

Can I live in the house during underpinning?

Usually yes, though it is noisy and untidy. Discuss it with the contractor.

How long does underpinning last?

A properly designed and built job should last for the life of the building. Check what guarantee is offered.
