If you have just noticed cracks

What to do first, and what to hold off on

A few calm steps in the first weeks protect your position with an insurer and make any later inspection easier.

Do

  • Photograph and measurePut a ruler beside each crack, photograph it with the date and measure the width.
  • Look for causesLarge trees, damp patches, gurgling drains and recent building work nearby.
  • Tell your insurerIf you have buildings insurance, report it early and before any repairs.
  • Keep a recordRe-measure every couple of weeks and write down what you see.
  • Get it assessedIf it is wider than about 3mm or growing, ask for a proper inspection.

Do not

  • Fill the cracks yetIt hides the evidence a surveyor and insurer need.
  • Cut down a nearby treeOn clay, removal can cause the ground to swell and make things worse.
  • Start repairs firstWork done before your insurer has looked can undermine a claim.
  • Ignore fast changeBulging walls or rapidly growing cracks need urgent advice.
  • PanicMost subsidence is slow, and many cracks are harmless.

Local knowledge

Why subsidence is a Leicester question

Much of Leicester sits on clay-rich ground, and clay swells when it is wet and shrinks when it is dry. Add mature trees, Victorian terraces with shallow foundations and a run of hot, dry summers, and the result is a steady flow of cracked walls. One analysis of 2025 insurance data ranked Leicester at the top of English cities for subsidence cases, at around 2.3 claims per 1,000 homes, and insurers have reported record subsidence payouts nationally.

The detail changes from street to street. South and east Leicester have leafy streets of Victorian villas and interwar semis. The terraces around the centre have shared walls and shared drains. Land near the River Soar has softer river deposits, and a few spots in the county have old workings. Read about the ground under Leicester houses and council trees and subsidence, or go straight to your area below.

These pages are general information. They are not a survey of your house, and only a proper inspection can say what is causing your cracks.

FAQ

Your questions answered

How do I know if my cracks are subsidence?

Cracks wider than about 3mm, diagonal or stepped cracks near doors and windows, doors that start to stick and cracks that grow over weeks or months all point towards it. Measuring over several months is the only reliable test. See reading cracks in your walls.

What causes subsidence in Leicester?

Most cases involve clay soil shrinking in dry weather, often made worse by trees close to the house. Leaking drains, old ground workings and poorly compacted ground are other causes.

Is subsidence covered by buildings insurance?

Usually, yes. Subsidence is a standard insured risk on most buildings policies, with an excess. Tell your insurer promptly and do not start repairs first.

No. This site takes enquiries and gives general information. Surveying, reporting and repair work are done by separate, independent firms.

How much does subsidence repair cost?

From a few hundred pounds for a drain survey to tens of thousands for underpinning. See typical costs.

Should I fill the cracks?

Not yet. Measure and photograph them first. Filling hides the evidence needed to tell whether the movement is continuing.
