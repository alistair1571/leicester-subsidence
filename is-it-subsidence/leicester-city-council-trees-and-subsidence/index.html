Council trees and subsidence in Leicester

Street trees add a great deal to Leicester's roads. When one is close to a house on clay, it can also be the cause of cracking.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Plenty of Leicester homes have a council-owned tree outside, on the pavement, a verge, in a park or in an open space. If your house is cracking and a large tree is close by, the owner of that tree matters, because it affects who deals with the problem and who may pay for it. This page covers Leicester City Council trees; if you live in Oadby, Wigston, Birstall, Glenfield or Blaby, a different council is responsible. See the pages on those areas under Oadby, Wigston, Birstall, Glenfield and Blaby.

Local background

Council trees and subsidence have come up repeatedly in Leicester. Homeowners have raised them in property listings and with the council's customer channels, and the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman has considered complaints about council trees and alleged damage to houses. It shows these cases are not unusual. Every case turns on its own evidence.

What to do if you think a council tree is involved

  1. Tell your buildings insurer first,if you have one. Insurers have experience handling these cases and may pursue the tree's owner themselves.
  2. Record the evidence.Photograph the tree, the distance to the house and every crack. Note the date and measure the cracks regularly.
  3. Report the issue to the councilthrough its website or customer service, giving the address, the tree's location and a short description. Ask for a reference number.
  4. Do not touch the tree.Cutting or damaging a council tree is an offence, and the council needs to assess it.
  5. Expect the council to ask for evidence.Councils usually need monitoring results, a drain survey and sometimes root identification and soil data before agreeing that a tree is the cause.

What evidence matters

  • Crack monitoring over at least a full season, ideally covering a dry summer and a wetter winter
  • Proof the movement is linked to the tree: for instance, cracks nearest the tree, a pattern of movement that follows the seasons and a soil with high shrink-swell potential
  • Evidence that other causes, particularly leaking drains, have been ruled out
  • Root identification showing roots under the foundations belong to the tree

What the council might do

If the evidence points to a tree, the council may reduce or remove it, or defend a claim. A tree that is protected or very significant may be treated more cautiously. Removing a tree can cause the clay to swell and push the house up, so any plan should be monitored. See Trees, Roots and House Movement.

If you are buying or selling a house near a council tree

Ask the seller about the tree and any cracks, and ask the surveyor to look at the distance and species. If you are selling, be open about what you know. See Buying a House with Cracks and Selling a House with Subsidence.

Frequently asked questions

Can I claim against Leicester City Council for tree damage?

In principle a tree's owner can be liable if its roots cause damage and the damage was foreseeable, but each case depends on evidence. Your insurer may handle this for you.

Can I prune a council tree myself?

No. You may not cut or damage a tree that belongs to the council. Report the issue and let the council assess it.

Who deals with trees in Oadby or Wigston?

Oadby and Wigston Borough Council is responsible for its own trees. In Birstall it is Charnwood Borough Council, and in Glenfield and Blaby it is Blaby District Council.
