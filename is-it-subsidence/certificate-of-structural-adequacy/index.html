Certificate of structural adequacy

A short document that can make or break a sale after subsidence. Here is what it is, who gives one and what to do without one.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

A certificate of structural adequacy (CSA) is a statement from a chartered structural engineer that a building, or part of it, is structurally sound following movement or repair. Buyers, lenders and insurers ask for one when a property has a history of subsidence or visible cracks.

What it usually says

  • That the engineer has inspected the property
  • That the building is structurally adequate for its use, based on the evidence
  • Often, that any remaining movement is not significant, or that repairs have restored stability
  • The engineer's name, qualifications and insurance details

It is a professional opinion based on the engineer's inspection and the evidence available at the time, not a guarantee that the building will never move again.

When you might need one

  • Selling a house that has had a subsidence claim
  • Buying a house with cracks where the lender wants reassurance
  • After underpinning, resin injection or other stabilisation work
  • When an insurer asks for confirmation before closing a claim or offering cover

How to get one

  1. Ask your insurer first,if there is an open claim. They may arrange this as part of closing it.
  2. Otherwise instruct a chartered structural engineer.Check their qualifications (for example, membership of the Institution of Structural Engineers) and that they carry professional indemnity insurance.
  3. Give them what you have: earlier reports, monitoring data, repair records and photographs.
  4. Expect a visit and sometimes more monitoring before they will sign.

If monitoring is not finished

An engineer is unlikely to certify a building that is still moving. They may recommend more monitoring first. See Crack Monitoring.

Cost

Fees vary with the engineer and the complexity of the property. Ask for a fixed quote that says what is included, and whether further monitoring would be charged separately.

Frequently asked questions

Who can issue a certificate of structural adequacy?

A chartered structural engineer. Check they are properly qualified and insured.

Is a CSA the same as a guarantee?

No. It is a professional opinion at a point in time. Repair guarantees are separate documents from the contractor.

Do I need one to sell?

Not legally, but buyers and lenders often ask for one after subsidence, and having it ready can speed a sale.
