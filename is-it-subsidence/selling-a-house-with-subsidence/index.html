Selling a house with subsidence

Selling after subsidence is entirely possible. Buyers and lenders want honesty and paperwork.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

A house with a history of subsidence can be sold, and plenty are. The trouble comes when a seller is vague, cannot show what was done or hopes the buyer will not notice. Be open from the start and gather the documents early.

What you must tell the buyer

In England, sellers complete a property information form that asks about movement and insurance claims. You must answer honestly. Concealing a known problem or giving a misleading answer can lead to a claim by the buyer after completion. Your conveyancer will guide you, and it is better to over-disclose.

Documents that help

  • The engineer's report and monitoring records
  • Details of the cause and what was done: tree work, drain repair, underpinning, resin injection and so on
  • A certificate of structural adequacy
  • Guarantees for the repair work, and whether they pass to a buyer
  • Building regulations sign-off if structural work was done
  • Your insurer's correspondence, including confirmation that the claim is closed

Selling if the problem is not yet solved

If monitoring is still going on or the cause is unknown, selling is harder. Many buyers need a mortgage, and lenders are cautious. Options include waiting until the claim is complete, selling to a cash buyer or specialist buyer who understands the issue, or lowering the price to reflect the risk. Take advice from your estate agent and solicitor.

How a past claim affects the price

A completed repair with clean paperwork usually has less effect than people fear, though some buyers will still be wary. An unresolved problem or poor records can hit the price hard. The more clearly you can show what happened and that it is over, the better.

Insurance for the buyer

Buyers will check they can get buildings cover. A broker can help, and having documents ready makes this faster. See Buying a House with Cracks for the buyer's side.

This is general information, not legal advice. Your solicitor can advise on what you must disclose in your situation.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to tell buyers about past subsidence?

Yes, if asked, and you must answer honestly. Your solicitor will go through the property information form with you.

Will I get a lower price?

Possibly, though a completed repair with documents often has less effect than people expect. An unresolved problem tends to hurt more.

Can I sell while a claim is open?

It is possible, but harder. Many buyers and lenders prefer to wait until the movement is understood and repairs are done.
