Mining and ground voids in Leicestershire

Most Leicester subsidence is about clay, trees and drains. Old workings are rarer, but where they exist the consequences can be sudden.

Mining left its mark across parts of Leicestershire. The best-known area is the Leicestershire coalfield in the north-west of the county, around the Coalville area. Under most of Leicester city, the usual causes are the ground, trees and drains rather than mine workings, but old shafts, shallow workings and filled ground can turn up in surprising places.

How voids differ from clay movement

  • Clay movement is gradual and seasonal. Cracks open and close with the weather.
  • Voids and shafts can fail suddenly, with a hole opening or an area dropping, often with no seasonal pattern.

Types of ground void

  • Old coal workings and shafts, mainly in the north-west of the county
  • Quarries and clay pits that have been filled in, leaving made ground that may settle
  • Prospecting shafts sunk to test for coal. One reported example in Leicester is a shaft from the 1700s that collapsed in Spinney Hills and left a hole several metres across. See Spinney Hills
  • Old drains, wells and cellars that were never recorded

How to check

  1. Coal mining searches.The Mining Remediation Authority (formerly the Coal Authority) holds records of known coal mining and publishes a map viewer showing mining areas. A coal mining report is part of the standard searches when you buy a house in a coal mining area.
  2. Check the local authority records and the BGS maps for known shafts, quarries and made ground.
  3. Ask a specialist if the cracks are sudden, or the ground has dropped without a seasonal pattern.

If a hole appears

Keep people and pets away, do not fill it and call the council or the emergency services depending on the risk. Tell your insurer. Voids and collapsed shafts are a different kind of problem from clay movement, and treatment is usually ground stabilisation or grouting designed by an engineer.

Insurance

Subsidence caused by mining is usually covered by buildings insurance in the same way as other subsidence, though the details depend on the policy. In some coalfield areas there are separate arrangements. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Frequently asked questions

Is Leicester built over old mines?

Not generally. The coalfield is mostly in the north-west of the county. Isolated shafts, quarries and filled ground do exist, so check records for your address.

Does house insurance cover mining subsidence?

Often yes under subsidence cover, but check your policy and ask your insurer.

How do I find out if there are mine workings under my house?

A coal mining report and the Mining Remediation Authority map viewer are the usual starting points, along with BGS and council records.
