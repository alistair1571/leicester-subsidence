Extensions, bays and garden walls

Parts of a house built at different times, or on shallower foundations, often move differently from the rest. That can look alarming and not always be serious.

Extensions, bay windows, porches and garden walls tend to have shallower foundations than the main house. That makes them more sensitive to what is happening in the top layer of ground, including seasonal shrinking and swelling of clay, nearby roots and leaking pipes.

Extensions

A vertical crack between an extension and the original house, running from top to bottom, is common. The two parts often sit on different foundations and move at different rates. If the gap is narrow and stable, it can be sealed with a flexible filler. If it is wide, growing or has a step in it, have it looked at.

  • Check whether the extension is on its own foundation or shares one with the house
  • Look for the crack opening more in summer than in winter
  • Note any trees or shrubs close to the extension, which may be near the foundations
  • Keep the building regulations completion certificate and plans; a surveyor will ask for them

Bay windows

Many Victorian and Edwardian terraces and semis around Leicester have bay windows. Bays often sit on shallower foundations than the main walls, and because they stick out, they carry weight on a smaller footprint. Cracks around the bay, at the junction with the main wall or in the brickwork above the window are common signs of movement. They are not always subsidence. Sometimes the cause is failing lintels or roof loading, so it is worth having an engineer look.

Garden walls, steps and patios

Boundary walls generally have very shallow foundations. Leaning walls, cracked copings and sunken steps are common in older gardens. They tell you less about the house than you might expect, but if the damage is close to the building, near a drain run or along the same side as a large tree, it may be the same cause as cracks in the house.

Other reasons a part of the building cracks

  • Thermal movement, particularly in south-facing brickwork and flat roofs
  • Wall ties corroding and pushing the outer leaf of a cavity wall out
  • Lintels rusting and expanding
  • Roof spread, where the roof pushes the top of a wall outwards

Is it covered by insurance?

Subsidence cover on buildings insurance usually includes extensions, but some policies treat garden walls, patios, drives and paths separately or exclude them. Check the wording, and see Claiming on Buildings Insurance for how a claim usually works.

What to do next

Photograph and measure the crack. Check for the signs listed in reading cracks in your walls. If you are buying a house with an extension that has cracked, see Buying a House with Cracks.

Frequently asked questions

Why do extensions crack away from the house?

They are built on separate and often shallower foundations, so they can move at a different rate from the original building.

Are bay window cracks serious?

They can be. Bays sit on shallow foundations and carry weight on a small footprint. A stable hairline crack is usually cosmetic, while a widening one is worth an inspection.

Does subsidence cover include garden walls?

Often not as standard. Many policies limit or exclude boundary walls, paths and patios, so check your policy wording.
