Quick answers

What is subsidence?

Subsidence is the downward movement of the ground under a building, caused by the soil losing volume or support. It can crack walls, jam doors and, in serious cases, affect the safety of the structure.

What causes subsidence in Leicester?

The usual causes are clay soil shrinking in dry weather, tree roots drawing water, leaking drains and, less often, old mine workings or made-up ground. Leicester has clay across much of the city, so tree and drought-related movement is common.

How do I know if it is subsidence?

Look for diagonal or stepped cracks wider than about 3mm, doors and windows that stick, and cracks that get wider over weeks or months. Monitoring over several months is the reliable way to confirm it.

Is subsidence covered by buildings insurance?

Usually yes. Subsidence is a standard insured risk on most policies, although there is normally an excess. Settlement and poor workmanship are generally not covered.

How much does it cost to fix?

It ranges widely. A drain repair or tree work may cost a few thousand pounds, while underpinning often costs well into five figures. See our guide to subsidence repair costs.

How long does a subsidence claim take?

Often many months, because insurers monitor movement for six to twelve months or longer to see what the ground is doing across the seasons.

Can I sell a house with subsidence?

Yes, but you have to answer buyers' questions honestly, and the sale is much easier if you can show the cause was dealt with and have documents such as a certificate of structural adequacy.

Will I be able to get insurance after a claim?

Usually, but premiums and excesses may rise. A broker can help you compare terms.

Does subsidence get worse?

It can if the cause is not dealt with. Cracks often grow in hot, dry spells, then stabilise when the ground is wet again, but they do not heal on their own.

Should I cut down a tree near my house?

Not without advice. On clay, removing a tree can cause the ground to swell (heave) and move the house in the other direction.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

In most cases, yes. Most subsidence is slow. If you see rapidly growing cracks, bulging walls or a leaning structure, get urgent advice from a structural engineer or your insurer.
