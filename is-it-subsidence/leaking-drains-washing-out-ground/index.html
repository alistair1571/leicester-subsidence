Leaking drains and washed-out ground

Water carries soil with it. A cracked drain under or near a house can quietly remove ground from beneath the foundations.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Most people picture subsidence as a tree problem. In many houses the culprit is a broken drain. Old clay pipes crack, joints slip and tree roots find their way in. Water escapes into the ground, carries fine soil away and leaves a void or a softened patch, and the foundations above it drop.

Signs a drain may be involved

  • Cracks that start near a corner of the house where a drain runs underneath
  • Gurgling sinks or toilets, slow-draining baths or repeated blockages
  • Damp, boggy or sunken patches in the garden or on the drive
  • Unexplained dips in paving or a sinking manhole cover
  • Smells coming from the garden or around gullies
  • Subsidence-type cracks in a house with no big trees close by

How it is checked

The standard method is a CCTV drain survey, where a small camera is fed along the pipes to look for cracks, displaced joints, roots, collapsed sections and signs of leaks. A dye test or a pressure test can also be used. Surveys are quick, and the report usually includes a recording and a plan, which insurers often ask for.

Who is responsible for a drain?

Responsibility depends on where the pipe is. As a general guide in England:

  • Pipes inside your boundary that serve only your property are normally your responsibility
  • Pipes outside your boundary, and shared drains and sewers, have been the responsibility of the water company since private sewers were transferred in 2011. In most of Leicestershire the sewerage company is Severn Trent Water
  • If a pipe serves several houses, check with the water company before you pay for anything

Buildings insurance often covers damage to the house from a leaking drain, and some policies also help with the cost of finding the leak. Check your policy.

Fixing a broken drain

Depending on the damage, a repair may involve patch lining, a full structural liner, replacing a section by digging, or in the worst cases rebuilding the run. A repaired drain does not stop the damage already caused. The ground may need stabilising, which is where Resin Injection or Underpinning can come in, but only if monitoring shows the house is still moving.

What to do next

If you spot the signs above, book a drain survey and keep the footage. Tell your insurer early, and read Claiming on Buildings Insurance before agreeing to any work.

Frequently asked questions

Can a leaking drain cause subsidence?

Yes. Leaking water can wash fine particles from the soil, leaving voids or soft ground that can no longer support the foundations.

Who pays for a drain survey?

If you claim on buildings insurance, your insurer may arrange and pay for one as part of the investigation. Otherwise you pay, and prices vary with the length of the drain run.

Is a blocked drain a sign of subsidence?

Not by itself, but repeated blockages in a house with new cracks make the drains worth surveying.
