Claiming on buildings insurance

Most buildings policies cover subsidence. How the claim goes depends on the evidence, the insurer's investigation and what you do in the first few weeks.

Subsidence is a standard insured risk on most UK buildings insurance, which means the insurer, not you, normally pays for investigation and repair once the claim is accepted. The process can take months, and some of what you do early on affects how smoothly it goes. This is general information, not advice about your policy.

Step by step

  1. Check your policy.Look for the subsidence section, the excess (often £1,000 or more for subsidence, and it can be higher) and any conditions. Check who the insurer is, since your mortgage lender or landlord may hold the policy.
  2. Tell the insurer promptly.Policies usually require you to report possible subsidence as soon as you are aware of it. Describe what you have seen, and send your photos and measurements.
  3. Do not start repairs or tree work.Wait until the insurer has had a chance to investigate. Repairs, filling cracks or felling trees before an inspection can undermine the claim.
  4. A loss adjuster is appointed.They arrange inspections, and often an engineer, drain survey, soil tests and crack monitoring. Monitoring commonly runs for a year or more.
  5. A cause is identifiedand the insurer decides what is needed: tree work, drain repair, underpinning or other stabilisation, followed by repairs and redecoration.
  6. Repairs and sign-off.When the structure is stable and repairs are done, ask for the documents that prove it, such as a certificate of structural adequacy.

Things worth knowing

  • Insurers differ. Some move quickly; others monitor for a long time before deciding.
  • You may have a say in who does the work in some cases. Ask, and keep everything in writing.
  • Future premiums and excesses can rise after a claim, and some insurers apply a higher subsidence excess to a property with a claims history. Ask what will change.
  • Settlement and poor workmanship are not normally covered. That is why identifying the cause matters.
  • Disputes can go to the insurer's complaints process and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which is free for consumers.

What the numbers look like

Figures from the Association of British Insurers have shown record subsidence payouts after hot summers, with total payouts in 2025 reported at around £307m and the average claim at about £20,000. Individual claims range from a few thousand pounds for tree and drain work to much larger sums where underpinning is needed.

If you are not sure it is subsidence

You can still tell the insurer, who will investigate. Or send an enquiry to find out what a specialist firm would recommend. Our enquiry service does not replace your insurer; if you have buildings insurance, they are the first call.

Frequently asked questions

Should I call my insurer or a surveyor first?

Usually your insurer. They may arrange and pay for investigation, and commissioning your own report first can complicate things. If in doubt, ask them.

How long does a subsidence claim take?

Often many months, sometimes more than a year, because insurers monitor movement over the seasons before deciding on repairs.

Will my premium go up?

It can. Insurers may raise the excess or the premium, or both, after a subsidence claim. Ask before you agree.

What if my insurer refuses?

Ask for their reasons in writing, then use their complaints process. After that you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which is free to use.
