Subsidence or settlement?

Both mean a building has moved down. The difference is whether the movement finished long ago or is still going on.

People use the words loosely, but they describe different things, and the difference matters for your insurer, your repairs and any future sale.

What settlement is

Settlement is the gradual sinking of a building as the ground beneath it compresses under the weight. It happens mostly in the first few years after a house is built, then stops. Cracks from settlement are usually small, stable and found in newer houses. Older homes that have not moved for decades are very unlikely to be settling now.

What subsidence is

Subsidence is downward movement of the ground under a building that is still going on, or has recently happened, because the soil has lost volume or support. The causes include:

  • Clay shrinking in dry weather or because of thirsty tree roots
  • Soil washed away by leaking drains or water mains
  • Old mine workings or other voids under the ground
  • Poorly compacted made-up ground

Subsidence tends to be uneven, so one part of the house drops more than another and cracks open as a result.

Heave, the reverse

Heave is when clay swells and pushes up. It most often happens after a large tree has been removed and the ground slowly takes water back in, and it can crack floors and walls much as subsidence does. A surveyor will consider it when looking at your house.

How the two compare

SettlementSubsidence
WhenMostly the first few years after constructionAny time, often after a dry summer or a change nearby
MovementSlow, then stopsContinues or comes and goes with the seasons
CracksFine, often even in widthWider, often diagonal and tapering
CauseSoil compressing under the building's weightSoil shrinking, washing away or collapsing
InsuranceNot normally coveredNormally covered on buildings insurance

How anyone finds out

The only reliable way to know is to measure. A specialist will fix small gauges (called tell-tales) or take level readings across the cracks and re-measure over several months. If the readings change, that is movement. If they stay the same, the crack is probably old. Crack monitoring usually runs for at least six to twelve months because of the seasonal pattern on clay.

What to do next

Record your cracks, avoid filling them, and look into whether you have trees, drains or recent building work nearby. If it looks active, tell your insurer or send an enquiry and have it assessed.

Frequently asked questions

How long does settlement take?

Most settlement happens in the first few years after a house is built. If your house is decades old and has just developed cracks, settlement is unlikely to be the explanation.

Can subsidence stop on its own?

Sometimes. If the cause was a dry summer, the ground may recover when it rains. If the cause is a tree, a drain or a void, it usually will not.

Is subsidence always covered by insurance?

Subsidence is a standard insured risk on most buildings policies, but policies differ, and there is usually an excess. Settlement and poor workmanship are generally not covered.
