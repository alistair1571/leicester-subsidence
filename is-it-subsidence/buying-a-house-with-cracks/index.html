Buying a house with cracks

Cracks do not always mean a bad buy, but they should always mean more questions. Here is what to ask, what to get checked and when to walk away.

A cracked wall can be a bargain or a money pit. The difference is whether the movement has stopped, whether the cause has been fixed and whether you can get insurance and a mortgage. Take time before you commit, because it is far cheaper to ask now than to find out after exchange.

Before you offer

  • Look at cracks inside and outside, and note any diagonal cracks, sticking doors and sloping floors
  • Look at the surroundings: large trees, hedges, drains and any recent extension work
  • Ask the agent whether the seller has made an insurance claim, and whether any repairs have been done
  • Check how old the cracks look. Dirty, painted-over cracks are old; clean-edged cracks are fresh

Get the right survey

A basic mortgage valuation is not a survey. For a house with visible cracks, ask for a RICS Level 3 building survey, or a structural engineer's inspection, and tell the surveyor about your concerns before they visit. They may recommend monitoring, a drain survey or further investigation, and it is worth doing before you are committed.

Questions for the seller

Sellers in England complete a property information form (usually the TA6) that asks about movement, subsidence and insurance claims. Ask to see the answers, and ask for:

  • Details of any previous subsidence claim, including the cause and what was done
  • Any monitoring records and engineer's reports
  • A certificate of structural adequacy for repair work
  • Guarantees for underpinning or other work, and whether they transfer to a new owner
  • Building regulations certificates for extensions

Insurance and mortgage

Talk to a broker or insurer about cover before exchange. A house with a history of subsidence can usually be insured, but the excess and premium may be higher, and some insurers will not offer terms without evidence the movement has stopped. A lender may also want a structural report before agreeing the mortgage.

Walk-away signs

  • The seller will not share claim or repair records
  • Cracks are active, the cause is unknown and the price has not reflected the risk
  • You cannot get buildings insurance at a reasonable cost
  • The survey recommends long-term monitoring and the seller wants a quick sale

Negotiating

If the survey finds a problem, you can ask for a price reduction, for the seller to carry out repairs with proper documentation, or for the sale to wait until monitoring is complete. Take advice from your conveyancer.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice. Your solicitor, surveyor and mortgage adviser are the right people to help with a specific purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a mortgage on a house that has had subsidence?

Often yes, if the cause has been dealt with and you have the paperwork to show it. Lenders and insurers will want evidence.

What survey should I ask for?

A RICS Level 3 building survey or an inspection by a structural engineer, with your concerns about cracks written into the instructions.

Will a cracked house be cheaper?

It can be, but price should reflect the cost and risk. A reduction is only a bargain if the problem is understood and fixable.
