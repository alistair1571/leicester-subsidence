Landlords and rented homes

If you rent out a house in Leicester and you see new cracks, here is what you owe tenants, what insurance usually covers and what tenants should do.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Leicester has a large private rented sector, much of it in older terraces and converted houses of the kind that often show movement. A cracked wall in a let property is a repair issue, an insurance issue and a tenant-relations issue at the same time.

What landlords are generally responsible for

In most tenancies in England, the landlord is responsible for the structure and exterior of the property, including walls, roof, foundations and drains, under section 11 of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985. Landlords must also make sure the home is fit for human habitation. The details depend on the tenancy, so take advice if you are unsure.

Landlord insurance

  • Most landlord buildings policies include subsidence cover, but check the excess and any conditions about unoccupied or poorly maintained properties
  • If the property is leasehold, the freeholder or management company usually insures the building. Report problems to them and check who is responsible for what
  • Tenants' belongings are not covered by your buildings policy, so tenants need their own contents cover

See Claiming on Buildings Insurance for how a claim works.

What a landlord should do

  1. Respond to the report.Visit the property, photograph the cracks and measure them. Keep a dated record.
  2. Tell your insurerpromptly, before repairs or tree work.
  3. Keep tenants informed.Explain what is happening, what access is needed and how long monitoring may take. Give proper notice for inspections.
  4. Check for urgent safety issues.Bulging walls, large rapid cracks or damage to gas or water pipes need an immediate response.
  5. Keep the paperwork from engineers, monitoring and repairs; a future buyer or lender will ask for it.

If you are a tenant

  • Tell your landlord or agent in writing as soon as you see new cracks, sticking doors or other movement, and keep a copy
  • Photograph the cracks with the date and a ruler beside them
  • Do not attempt repairs yourself, and do not fill cracks, because it can interfere with monitoring
  • Check your contents insurance for cover if belongings are damaged
  • If you are concerned about the safety of the property or the response, contact the council's private housing team, or Shelter or Citizens Advice for guidance

Selling or re-letting after a claim

Keep records of the cause, repairs and any certificate. They help when you remortgage, sell or re-let. See Selling a House with Subsidence.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays for subsidence repairs in a rented house?

Normally the landlord, usually through their buildings insurance. Tenants are not responsible for structural repairs unless they caused the damage.

Can a tenant stay in the house during monitoring?

Often yes, unless there is a safety concern. The landlord should explain what is happening and arrange inspections with proper notice.

Do landlords have to tell tenants about subsidence?

Landlords should respond to reported problems and keep tenants informed. If you have specific legal questions, take advice from a solicitor or housing adviser.
