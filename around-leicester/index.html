Subsidence around Leicester

Area guides covering the city and nearby towns, with local housing, ground and council details.

Send a free enquiry

Leicester covers many different kinds of ground and housing, and the councils change at the city boundary. These pages explain what is distinctive about each area and which council deals with trees and local services. They are general guides, not surveys of your street.

Leicester city

Knighton

LE2. Knighton's tree-lined roads of Victorian, Edwardian and 1930s houses sit on the south-east side of the city. Big trees and clay are the combination to watch.

Read more

Stoneygate

LE2. Stoneygate's large Victorian and Edwardian villas and mature trees make it one of the more distinctive parts of the city, and one where tree rules matter.

Read more

Clarendon Park

LE2. Clarendon Park's densely built Victorian terraces are popular with students and young professionals. Bay-fronted houses, shared drains and rented homes make it a common place for subsidence questions.

Read more

Evington

LE5. Evington is a mix of older housing and 1930s-onwards suburbs on the east side of the city, with plenty of gardens, hedges and mature trees.

Read more

Aylestone

LE2. Aylestone sits beside the River Soar and the canal on the south-west side of the city. River deposits and drains are the questions to ask here.

Read more

Highfields

LE2 and LE5. Highfields is built up with Victorian terraces close to the centre. Shared walls, shared drains and a high proportion of rented homes shape how subsidence issues play out.

Read more

Spinney Hills

LE5. Spinney Hills is a dense area of Victorian terraces east of the city centre, with an unusual piece of local history: a reported collapse of an old shaft.

Read more

Westcotes

LE3. Westcotes, west of the city centre, has Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas around Western Park, close to the river and canal.

Read more

Braunstone

LE3. Braunstone is a large estate begun in the 1920s and 1930s, with many former council homes. Estates like this raise their own questions about trees, drains and who owns what.

Read more

Belgrave

LE4. Belgrave is a busy area north of the centre, with Victorian terraces and the River Soar nearby.

Read more

Humberstone

LE5. Humberstone, to the east of Leicester, combines an older village area with a lot of 1930s semi-detached housing.

Read more

Beaumont Leys

LE4. Beaumont Leys is a large planned estate on the north-west edge of the city, mostly built from the 1970s, with a shopping centre and plenty of open space.

Read more

Rushey Mead

LE4. Rushey Mead is a suburb on the north-east side of Leicester with a lot of interwar and later semi-detached housing.

Read more

Oadby and Wigston

Oadby

LE2. Oadby is a leafy suburb just outside the city, with its own council. Mature gardens and large trees are part of its character.

Read more

Wigston

LE18. Wigston, south of Leicester, has an old village core, Victorian terraces and later housing, with clay deposits that matter for foundations.

Read more

Charnwood and Blaby

Birstall

LE4. Birstall is just north of the city in the Soar valley, in a different council area from Leicester.

Read more

Glenfield

LE3. Glenfield sits on the western edge of the city, in Blaby District, with an older village core and housing from several periods.

Read more

Blaby

LE8. Blaby, south-west of Leicester, is the centre of Blaby District, with a village core and newer estates.

Read more

Postcodes

The LE postcode area covers the city and much of the county. LE1 is the city centre, LE2 mainly the south and south-east (including Knighton, Stoneygate, Clarendon Park, Aylestone and Oadby), LE3 the west (Westcotes, Braunstone, Glenfield), LE4 the north and north-east (Belgrave, Rushey Mead, Beaumont Leys and Birstall), LE5 the east (Evington, Humberstone, Spinney Hills), LE8 Blaby and Whetstone and LE18 Wigston. If your area is not listed, send an enquiry anyway.
