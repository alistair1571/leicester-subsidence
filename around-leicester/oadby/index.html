Subsidence in Oadby

Oadby is a leafy suburb just outside the city, with its own council. Mature gardens and large trees are part of its character.

Send a free enquiry How this service works

Oadby lies south-east of Leicester, in the borough of Oadby and Wigston. It has well-established suburban housing with large gardens and many mature trees, plus student accommodation linked to the University of Leicester.

The ground and the houses

South-east of the city, the ground includes clay and glacial deposits, and large trees in well-planted suburbs are a common cause of shrink-swell movement. Houses of the interwar and post-war period often have modest foundations.

This is a general picture of the area, not a survey. Ground and construction can change from one street to the next, and only an inspection of your own house can show what is happening. The British Geological Survey maps are free to check; see Clay and Ground Under Leicester Houses.

What tends to matter in Oadby

  • Oadby and Wigston Borough Council, not Leicester City Council, is responsible for its own trees and many local services
  • Mature garden and street trees
  • Extensions and conservatories added to suburban houses
  • Student and rented homes, where landlords need to act on reports

Which council and who deals with drains

Oadby is not in the city of Leicester. Oadby and Wigston Borough Council is responsible for the council-owned trees and many local services here, not Leicester City Council. Sewers and drains beyond your property boundary are normally the water company's responsibility, which for most of Leicestershire is Severn Trent Water; drains inside your boundary serving only your home are usually yours. See Leaking Drains and Washed-Out Ground.

Postcodes

Oadby is mainly in the LE2 postcode area. If you are not sure whether we cover your street, send an enquiry anyway.

If you see cracks

  1. Measure and photograph every crack with a date, and do not fill it. See Reading Cracks in Your Walls.
  2. Tell your buildings insurer promptly if you have cover. See Claiming on Buildings Insurance.
  3. Look at trees and drains close to the cracks.
  4. Send an enquiry if you want one independent firm covering Oadby to look at it.

Which council do I contact about a tree in Oadby?

Oadby and Wigston Borough Council covers Oadby and Wigston. Tell your insurer first, then the council with evidence.
